My final project from studying abroad in Korea.

RX Energy was one of my final projects from my study abroad in Korea. It was a two month, really intensive brand identity program, where we visited museums, talked to brands, and got to see how they actually present their work.

The whole scope of the project was to combine different cultures to make a brand, so getting to be immersed in both Western and Eastern culture at the same time was super cool. This is the brand I ended up making: an energy drink for both Asian and Western audiences that feels authentic to both, and not just like a mashup of the two.

I blended traditional Korean elements with modern design, so it feels fresh and relatable, and like it could be on a shelf anywhere in the world.

Why an energy drink?

Honestly, back then I needed caffeine all the time (and I still do). But when I got to Korea, I realized it didn’t really have the energy drinks I was used to in America, like Celsius or Alani, the really good, flavorful ones. Most of what I found was pretty plain, and I just wasn’t used to it.

So I wanted to make the energy drink I was missing: one with fun flavors that bring Asian and Western traditions together, made with natural ingredients like green tea caffeine, probiotics and vitamins, that doesn’t look like every other can in the fridge.

Starting with research.

Before designing anything, I wanted to understand who actually drinks this stuff and why. So I sent out a quick survey to my classmates in Korea, and dug into how people there drink caffeine.

What my survey said

57%drink caffeine every single day
57%get most of it from coffee, and 29% from energy drinks
86%drink it just to have energy through the day
57%would want an energy drink with probiotics, and 43% with vitamins

What I learned about Korea

01Coffee comes firstKoreans have mostly relied on coffee to stay awake, usually two to three cups a day, and energy drinks weren’t really around until about 2010.
02Strict caffeine rulesSince 2013, high caffeine drinks can’t be sold in school zones, and strong ones need warning labels, so teens drink a lot less of them.
03But it’s growingEnergy drink sales jumped over 50% in two years with young adults, and brands are switching to less caffeine and more natural ingredients.

All of this pointed me in the same direction: an energy drink made for young adults, with gentler green tea caffeine, probiotics and vitamins, and flavors fun enough to compete with coffee.

One can, three flavors.

Each can gets its own color based on its flavor. Behind it is a skyline of Korea’s landmarks that I sketched myself in Procreate, from traditional architecture to Seoul Tower, so every can carries a little bit of Seoul with it.

The white brush stroke is all about energy and movement, and the three benefits (green tea caffeine, vitamins and probiotics) sit right on the front so you see them right away.

Lychee

Sweet and floral with a little hint of citrus, inspired by a fruit that’s loved all over Asia. It’s light and refreshing, for when you want something a little different.

Muscat

Juicy and sweet like fresh green grapes, with a soft floral finish. Crisp and a little fancy.

Orange

Bright, zesty and a little tangy. The classic one that works any time of day.

A pop up room for every flavor.

Korea is known for its pop up stores, so I wanted RX to launch with one. Each flavor gets its own room, designed around its color and personality, so walking through it feels like stepping inside the can.

Lychee room

Soft, floral and pink, like stepping right into the lychee itself.

Muscat room

Lush and green, built around the juicy grape flavor.

Orange room

Bright, bold and impossible to miss, just like the flavor.
